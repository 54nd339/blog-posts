# Reference blogs

The house style is a blend of these. When a style question isn't answered by the other reference files, ask "what would these writers do?"

## Explainer tradition (maths, physics, and the "idea" half of CS posts)

- **Better Explained — Kalid Azad** (betterexplained.com). Intuition-first teaching via the **ADEPT** method: Analogy → Diagram → Example → Plain-English → Technical definition. "Blurry to sharp." Aha moments over memorised procedure. This is the backbone of our maths/physics section structure.
- **3Blue1Brown — Grant Sanderson**. The interplay of *concreteness and abstraction*: start at the lowest level of abstraction with a concrete, ideally visual case, then move up. A specific 3×3 matrix before "an n×n matrix".
- **Quanta Magazine**. Narrative science journalism — "braids the complexities of science with the art of storytelling", takes hard ideas seriously without dumbing them down, uses character and scene (Alice and Bob at the black hole). Our ledes borrow the "open on a concrete scene or puzzle" move.
- **Math with Bad Drawings — Ben Orlin**. Humour and hand-drawn figures that serve the maths, never bury it. Our "one playful touch, and it still tells you the subject" rule.

## Engineering tradition (algorithms, systems, tools, interview)

- **Julia Evans** (jvns.ca). Learns in public from first principles; "remembers what it felt like to not understand something"; zero condescension; short, concrete, curious. Zines and comics that explain rather than decorate. Our reader model and jargon rule come straight from here.
- **Phil Eaton** (notes.eatonphil.com, "What makes a great tech blog"). Show working code, not overviews. Tackle the confusing topics people hand-wave. Be transparent about tradeoffs and about what you don't know. Skip slang, memes, sarcasm, rants — write plainly for a global audience.
- **Dan Luu** (danluu.com). Data over vibes; real measurements; claims backed by numbers; willing to be long only when the evidence needs it.
- **Bartosz Ciechanowski** (ciechanow.ski). The gold standard for building an explanation up from a picture — even without interactivity, the discipline of "motivate every element before you introduce it" is the lesson.
- **fasterthanli.me — Amos Wenger**. Conversational, patient, willing to follow a thread all the way down. A reminder that "human" can also be thorough.

## Trends worth knowing (2025–2026)

- **Explorable explanations** (Bret Victor's coinage; Nicky Case, Ciechanowski, Sanderson). Interactive widgets aren't in scope for a Markdown repo, but the underlying value — let the reader *do* the idea, not just read it — informs the "walk one concrete example all the way through" rule.
- **Anti-"AI slop" reaction**. Readers now actively distrust prose that over-uses em dashes, rhetorical triplets, "it's not just X, it's Y", empty rhetorical questions, and vague uplift. The defence is *specificity* and a *point of view* — see `voice-and-style.md` §8.
- **Length**: SEO data clusters the "sweet spot" around 1,500–2,500 words (5–8 min), but the consistent finding is that structure and search-intent fit beat raw length. Our per-family targets in `post-structure.md` are set for the *content*, not for a word count.
- **Humour in technical writing**: the community consensus is "relevant, subtle, sparing, strategic"; never in warnings, definitions, or results; keep it translatable for non-native readers.
